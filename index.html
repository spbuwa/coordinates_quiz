How coordinates work

Every place on Earth has an address made of two numbers. The first says how far north or south it is. The second says how far east or west. Together they pin the place down to a point on the map.

Latitude

Measures distance north or south of the Equator, the imaginary line around the middle of the Earth.

Runs from 0° at the Equator to +90° at the North Pole and −90° at the South Pole. Lines of latitude run east to west.

Longitude

Measures distance east or west of the Prime Meridian, the line that passes through Greenwich in London.

Runs from 0° at the Prime Meridian to +180° eastward and −180° westward, meeting in the Pacific Ocean. Lines of longitude run north to south.

The sign tells you the side

The quizzes write coordinates with plus and minus signs. Plus means north or east. Minus means south or west. Use this chart to turn the signs into hemispheres.

That makes the Northern and Eastern Hemispheres. On the map, it is Tokyo, in Asia.

  • Latitude always comes first, then longitude.
  • Maps often use letters instead of signs. 40.71° N, 74.01° W is the same place as +40.71°, −74.01°.
  • Numbers close to 0 mean the place is near the Equator or the Prime Meridian. Check the sign carefully there.
  • A continent can lie in more than one hemisphere. Africa, for example, spans both north and south.
  • At the poles, longitude stops mattering, because every meridian meets there.
